# Writing Voice

Status: PROVISIONAL — no previous LinkedIn posts supplied yet.

This is the single source of truth for voice. Every skill reads this file;
there is no global `~/.claude/linkedin/voice.md` for this project.

## Desired style
Practical, clear, technically credible and experience-driven.

## Initial guidance
- Write like an engineer sharing something learned.
- Prefer concrete examples over broad claims.
- Explain why a technical decision matters.
- Use straightforward language.
- Avoid exaggerated hooks and influencer language.
- Avoid generic motivational conclusions.
- Do not force jokes, controversy or engagement bait.
- Keep paragraphs readable on mobile.
- Use technical terms when they help precision; explain less
  familiar terms when needed.

## Evidence needed
Ask the user for 3–5 examples of their own LinkedIn posts.
Use them to refine:
- Sentence length and rhythm
- Vocabulary and expressions
- Level of technical detail
- Use of first person
- Hook and closing style
- Formatting and paragraph length
- Phrases the user likes or dislikes

Do not claim that the agent has learned the user's authentic
voice until real examples have been reviewed.

## What the reference posts show (limited evidence)
- Short. One to three lines; no long-form technical post exists yet.
- Casual and warm, first person, contractions.
- Dry, light humour aimed at the industry, not at people (post 1). That post
  is the closest thing to a natural voice sample: a wry observation ending in
  a real question.
- Occasional single emoji and exclamation mark; ellipsis as a pause.
- No jargon stacking, no lists, no "lessons" framing, no hard sell.

## What they do not show

project story. So for technical drafts the voice is still an informed guess:
keep it plain and conversational, allow one dry aside, and prefer shorter
posts than the upstream 900-1,300 character default (aim for 600-1,000)
until the user's edits say otherwise. Record the user's edits to drafts here
as further voice evidence.
